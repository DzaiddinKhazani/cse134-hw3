Music in Media: A Ramble

By Dzaiddin Khazani

It’s such a nothingburger take to say that music is one of the most important aspects of a piece of media. This is because the elevation and evocation of a specific emotion that you want the audience to feel at a moment is so easy to coax when you use the brain’s pattern recognition to do its work for you. From the desert levels using a lot of strings and flutes, to the conveyance of the majesty and greatness of space with its orchestra and choirs, or the imaging of a specific period like the 80s with synths and 808 samples. Our brains are just so easy to trick with sounds that it’s amazing! That’s why I think a great OST is KEY to being a memorable piece of media! When you just listen to a specific track and you jump back to that one beautiful scene, that’s the mark of something special.

I loved Esoteric Ebb so much, and spent hours vibing around Norvik, groovying to Waterlane, or when I’m bored, chilling at the Goblin Throne room (my favorite track by far). But besides the banger area tracks, no amount of words could describe how safe and relaxing I feel when Ragn and his trusty goblin companion, nay, friend Snell, take a break, and the latter’s theme starts playing. Dear Snell means that you are safe, because you are not alone, never alone, not when Snell is by your side. After months of finishing Esoteric Ebb, I queued up Dear Snell for the first time today while walking to the library, and I couldn’t help but smile! The track actually coaxed me to relax, take a breather, because your friend is here right by you, and he’s going to use his sling to give you advantage on any combat checks!

Fast forward a couple of hours later, I was listening to the Project Hail Mary soundtrack as background noise while studying, and when the choir crescendoed at the climax of The Moment, I was again travelling back in time to the theaters, where I felt like I was right besides Ryland Grace basking in the infrared glow of the Petrova Line. It was such a magical moment that it really does stick with you, for oh so long, and it only takes one song to take you back there.

I can go on for hours on more of these specific moments that come to mind, like the main riff of Pacific Rim, the Dies Irae leitmotif in Sweeney Todd, Pascal’s Village in Nier: Automata or Rey Dau’s Theme in MH: Wilds to name a few. But I think my point is still the same. Queue up the OST of your favorite piece of media, and transport yourself to that magical moment, the memory that your brain has chained together with these specific order of sound waves, and have a blast to the past for the next 4 to 5 minutes!
